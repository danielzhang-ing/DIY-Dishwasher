# DIY-Dishwasher

By Sophie Barrowman and Daniel Zhang

Objective:
To design a fully functioning dishwasher with a capability to clean a day’s worth of dirty dishware.
To design a (relatively) food safe, water and budgetarily efficient dishwasher while documenting the process to deliver a high quality open source project that anyone can follow and learn from.
To use the core tenets of engineering to design, manufacture, and test a working prototype.
To explore and overcome the challenges associated with sourcing components, materials, and assembling them with intent for safety.
To collect our findings and to organize it in a manner so that it is presentable to a general audience while in a professional, technical manner that maintains its educational and entertainment value.



Background & Research:
Our inspiration comes from the fact that we, two very busy engineering students at UCLA, didn’t have time to do the dishes. Our apartment in sophomore year thankfully had a dishwasher, but with our lease secured for a studio in the following year, we realized that despite the rent being over $2000/month, it didn’t come with a dishwasher. So, as engineers do, we decided to make something that will automate the process of doing the dishes for us (even if the amount of blood, sweat, and dollars we put into making this outweighs doing the dishes ourselves).

Our starting point is with Billy Cheung’s DIY blog online. From what we could find, he published the most complete and detailed process of making a dishwasher from scratch. Their work and process is what we want to continue and improve on. From there, we decided that we wanted to start from scratch and build the entire dishwasher from the ground up. A full-sized dishwasher is too ambitious (and expensive) thus we decided on a reasonable size to be something similar to a countertop or portable dishwasher. These can also fit about a day’s worth of dishes in them, which works perfectly fine for us. 

From our personal experience and findings online about dishwashers, the main mechanisms of cleaning come from hot water and detergent breaking down fats, oils, and washing away anything stuck on to the dishware. Not only that, but running a full cycle within a dishwasher involves multiple steps, such as the heating, prewash, deploying detergent, draining, and drying. This means we need a rigorous control schema with various timers and sensors to ensure a safe and effective clean.
